## Super Nóva

- Explosion of a star
- Energy emission of $10^{51}$ erg or $10^{44}$ Joule
- $99 \%$ in form of, neutrinos
- $20-30$ SN explosions per second in the universe
- 3 SN explosion per 100 years in our galaxy


## Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN)

- Active galactic nuclei (AGNs) are compact regions in the centers of galaxies that appear very bright in electromagnetic radiation
- Regions contain supermassive black holes that accrete surrounding matter, converting gravitational energy into radiation
- In-falling matter forms an accretion disk. In many AGNs, relativistic jets perpendicular to the disk are observed
- Example: Quasar, Blazar


## Gamma Ray Bursts (GRB)

- GRBs are extremely energetic explosions observed in distant galaxies
- For a period of $10^{-3}$ to $10^{3}$ seconds they outshine every other source of gamma rays in the Universe
- They were first detected by a set of military satellites, operated by the US Air Force
- Long bursts (duration > 2s): associated with the core-collapse of a supermassive star
- Short bursts ( $<2 \mathrm{~s}$ ): result of two merging neutron stars, or the merging of a neutron star and a black hole
